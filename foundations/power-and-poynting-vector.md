# Electromagnetic Power and the Poynting Vector

The Poynting vector describes electromagnetic energy flux.

$$
\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H}.
$$

Its direction gives the local direction of electromagnetic energy transport, while its magnitude has units of power per unit area.

For time-harmonic fields, the time-average power-flow density is commonly written

$$
\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Re}\left\{\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H}^{*}\right\}.
$$

This concept connects field theory directly to practical quantities such as antenna power density, waveguide power, RF exposure, radar illumination, and wireless-link power flow.
