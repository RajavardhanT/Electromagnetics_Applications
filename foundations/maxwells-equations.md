# Maxwell's Equations

Maxwell's equations provide the common foundation for essentially every topic in this collection.

## Differential form

### Gauss's law

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{D}=\rho$$

Electric charge acts as a source or sink of electric flux.

### Gauss's law for magnetism

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}=0$$

Magnetic field lines form continuous closed paths; isolated magnetic monopoles are not part of classical electromagnetism.

### Faraday's law

$$\nabla\times\mathbf{E}=-\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t}$$

A time-varying magnetic field produces a circulating electric field. This principle underlies transformers, generators, inductive sensors, and wireless power transfer.

### Ampère-Maxwell law

$$\nabla\times\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{J}+\frac{\partial\mathbf{D}}{\partial t}$$

Electric current and time-varying electric flux generate magnetic fields.

## Why these equations matter

Together with material relations such as

$$\mathbf{D}=\epsilon\mathbf{E},\qquad \mathbf{B}=\mu\mathbf{H},\qquad \mathbf{J}=\sigma\mathbf{E},$$

Maxwell's equations lead to the electromagnetic wave equation and explain antennas, transmission lines, waveguides, radar, optics, wireless communications, electromagnetic interference, and many other technologies.

## Application chain

**Maxwell's equations → wave equation → propagation → interaction with materials and boundaries → electromagnetic devices → engineering systems**
